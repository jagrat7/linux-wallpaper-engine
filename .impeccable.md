## Design Context

### Users
Linux desktop users — from casual to power users — who own Wallpaper Engine on Steam and want to browse, manage, and apply animated wallpapers on Linux. They use this app during desktop customization sessions, often in the evening or late at night in a dimly lit room. The core loop is: browse wallpapers visually, preview them, apply one, maybe build a playlist. It's a leisure activity, not a productivity tool.

### Brand Personality
**Playful, immersive, and discover-first.**

The wallpapers ARE the interface. The app should feel like a window into a gallery of living art — glass surfaces, translucent layers, and the current wallpaper bleeding through the UI create a sense that the content and the chrome are one. The experience should spark the excitement of finding something new and the tactile pleasure of tinkering with your desktop setup.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **Tone**: Immersive-translucent. The UI is a lens over the wallpaper, not a container around it. Glass backgrounds, translucent panels, and the active wallpaper visible beneath the chrome are the signature.

- **References**: The app's existing glass utility and wallpaper-background integration are the right instinct — lean into this further. Think: a frosted window floating over a living scene.
- **Anti-references**: Generic admin dashboards, flat Material Design, anything that feels like a settings panel rather than a visual browsing experience. Avoid heavy opaque chrome that hides the wallpaper beneath it.
- **Color**: OKLCH palette already in place. Neutrals should stay low-chroma so wallpaper colors take center stage. Accent colors should be restrained — the wallpapers provide all the color the UI needs.
- **Existing assets**: Logo at `assets/transparent-logo.png`, 4 theme variants (light, dark, hard-light, steam), glass utility, OKLCH token system.

### Design Principles

1. **The wallpaper is the hero.** Every design decision should defer to the content. If a UI element competes with the wallpaper for attention, reduce the element, not the wallpaper. Glass and translucency exist to let the content breathe through.

2. **Discovery over efficiency.** This is a browsing experience, not a task manager. Favor visual richness, generous thumbnails, and serendipitous exploration over dense data tables and compact lists. Make users want to scroll.

3. **Playful tactility.** Interactions should feel physical and responsive — snappy hover states, smooth transitions, satisfying feedback. The app should feel like something you enjoy touching, not just using.

4. **Restrained chrome, vivid content.** Keep the UI's own palette muted and translucent. Let the wallpapers inject all the color. The interface is the frame, never the painting.

5. **Distinct but familiar.** Inspired by Steam's ecosystem (users come from there) but with its own identity. Sharper, more refined, less heavy-handed than Steam's blue industrial aesthetic.
